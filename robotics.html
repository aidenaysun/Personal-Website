Control & Software

Odometry-based autonomous pathing

Our early autonomous used timed, stop-and-go position drives that were slow and imprecise. I replaced them by logging the robot's global pose (x, y, θ) from odometry and IMU data, plotting those runs, and generating optimized paths from the plots. A continuous drive-to-position controller then follows the path, which cut runtime and improved precision. The system was used across our 2023–2025 seasons.

Autonomous configuration menu

In the 2023 season our autonomous had to coordinate with our alliance partner's in a shared space, so parameters like start and truss delays, park location, detected color, and which program to run had to be adjustable before each match. I built a menu interface as a finite state machine that cycles through six entries with bounded wrap-around, using modular indexing and switch cases for context-sensitive control. It let us adapt to field conditions and partner strategies quickly, was reused in the 2024–2025 season, and helped our team reach the FTC State Championship.

Vision

Our robots used OpenCV and TensorFlow for detection. When our color detection failed under inconsistent lighting at a league championship, it led directly to the spectral sensing research.

Mechanism Design & Analysis

CAD

  • Intake and claw (2023–2024): a gearbox-driven claw with induced-fit, pyramid-shaped fingers, mounted on a servo mechanism with three degrees of freedom so it can orient to randomly placed game elements.
  • Flywheel shooter: a flywheel with an encoder-equipped Axon servo "flapper" linkage to adjust launch angle, and mounting points for a lazy-susan turret.
  • Spindexer: a 12-inch rotating indexer with custom walls and an integrated kicker, designed for the 18-inch size limit, with space reserved for color sensors for automatic sorting. I checked kinematic clearances for the full assembly with Fusion 360 motion studies.

Flywheel sizing

I sized the flywheel by modeling it as a solid disk: moment of inertia I = ½mr², stored energy E = ¼mr²ω², and matching that energy to the energy needed to bring the ball to its target speed (½mbvb²). That let me choose mass and radius within the geometric constraints, and I verified the radius by modeling the ball's path along the ramp in Fusion 360.

Torque and motor selection

For linear actuators I computed the minimum required torque from F = ma and the lead screw radius, τ = m·a·rs / η, accounting for mechanical efficiency. I matched gearbox ratios to the torque-speed balance and settled on a 435 RPM motor, minimizing losses and heat instead of picking by trial and error.
